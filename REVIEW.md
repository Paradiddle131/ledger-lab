# Review

The paths whose change takes the sensitive review tier, and the lenses each adds, beside the floor the `review` skill always applies.
The list names the files at the core of three areas, person hashing, access, and the spend and project fences, which always take the sensitive tier.
It is not every file that can affect them: a change elsewhere that touches personal data, a grant or spend is raised to sensitive by the lead or a reviewer, as the `review` skill's tier rule says.
The floor is sensitive whatever this file says: agent instruction and rule files such as `AGENTS.md`, `CLAUDE.md` and this file, hook folders, and `docs/agents/**`; it adds no lenses.
The skill's `tier.sh` reads the list below from the default branch; with no `## Routine` list, prose documentation outside the floor and these paths is routine (gates only), and every other path is standard (one reviewer and a verifier).
Each item is backticked globs and its lenses only, a form every version of `tier.sh` parses; `## What each item guards` gives the reason for each, in the same order.

## What each item guards

1. Person hashing and scrubbing: the rule that a vendor or payee is a person unless the evidence says otherwise, the keyed hash and the free-text scrub; the Chicago, UK and GLEIF steps that apply them; the fetch kit's refusal of a source flagged as naming people with no such step; the Chicago and UK payment models that flag a hashed payee, the payments mart and its unit test that hold back a hashed payee's voucher number, and the legal-entity match beside hashed names; the dbt tests listed; and the committed Chicago, UK and vendor samples of real rows; with their tests.
2. Access: the persona catalogue and its schema, the persona identities the access tests act as, the grants and the BigQuery datasets, accounts and policy tags generated from them, the column tags and the masked view their masked readers read, the visibility matrix, the aggregates and the views that serve aggregate readers, the purposes' views, the row access policies and the access review, with their tests.
3. Spend and project fences: the lab project's definitions (its budget, quotas, kill switch and landing bucket), the spend fences read offline, the module that names the lab's own project and the rule lines held to it, the dbt profile that reads the same variable and caps the billed bytes of the jobs it runs, the warehouse loader, the bucket landing, the payments contract's BigQuery check, the BigQuery parity check, and the agent's per-turn billed-bytes cap and its BigQuery mode, with their tests.
